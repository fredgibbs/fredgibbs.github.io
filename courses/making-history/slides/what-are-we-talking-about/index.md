Thursday · 7.2 · scale
{: .eyebrow}

## You cannot zoom in and out at the same time
{: .main-point}

<div class="cards" markdown="1">
<div class="card" markdown="1">

###### Zoom out · Braudel
{: .num}

#### Four centuries of a sea

Gets: why conventions last, what no one could change. Loses: the afternoon, the decision, the name.
</div>
<div class="card" markdown="1">

###### Zoom in · Ginzburg
{: .num}

#### One miller's trial

Gets: a mind in full, in his own words. Loses: any claim about how many others thought it.
</div>
</div>

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="0" markdown="1">

**Zoom out and you can explain everything and decide nothing. Zoom in and you can decide everything and explain almost nothing.**
</div>

Each scale buys one thing by giving up another. So what would it take for one of them to count as an improvement on the last?
{: .question.compact.fragment data-fragment-index="1"}
</section>

## Each turn fixes what the last one missed
{: .main-point}

<div class="flow" markdown="1">
<div class="step" markdown="1">

###### 01 · 1824
{: .num}

#### Against speculation

Voltaire judged the past by taste. Ranke will say only what a document can show.
</div>
<div class="step" markdown="1">

###### 02 · 1848
{: .num}

#### Underneath the archive

Marx reads underneath Ranke's states and princes, to who was producing what they ran on.
</div>
<div class="step" markdown="1">

###### 03 · 1963
{: .num}

#### Class as something people do

Marx's class is a structure. Thompson makes it something people do to each other.
</div>
<div class="step" markdown="1">

###### 04 · 1976
{: .num}

#### One man instead of a crowd

Thompson's weavers arrive in numbers; Ginzburg gets one man's cosmos in his own words.
</div>
</div>

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="0" markdown="1">

**Each turn answers a fairly specific complaint about the one before it. Whether four of those in a row add up to progress is a different question.**
</div>

<section markdown="1">
Weeks 3 and 4 · Al-Biruni and Ranke
{: .eyebrow}

## Al-Biruni had the test eight centuries early
{: .main-point}

<div class="parallel compact fragment" data-fragment-index="0" markdown="1">
<div class="track" markdown="1">

##### Al-Biruni · *Kitab al-Hind*, c. 1017

"No one will deny that in questions of historic authenticity hearsay does not equal eyewitness... How could we know the history of nations but for *the everlasting monuments of the pen*?" <span class="cite">preface</span>
</div>
<div class="track" markdown="1">

##### Ranke · *Geschichten der romanischen und germanischen Völker*, 1824

"History has had assigned to it the office of judging the past and of instructing the present... To such high offices the present work does not presume: it seeks only to show what actually happened." <span class="cite">introduction (GHDI trans.)</span>
</div>
</div>

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="1" markdown="1">

**Ranke made the refusal new. The test underneath it was already eight centuries old, which is odd for a sequence supposed to run forward.**
</div>
</section>

## Progress or accumulation
{: .main-point}

---

Is there any "progress" in historical writing? Are we now closer to the truth, or just accumulating competing frameworks?
{: .question .fragment data-fragment-index="1"}

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="2" markdown="1">
Real progress on narrow questions — whose sources, which test, what counts as evidence. The big one stays open, mostly because almost nobody agrees what the target is.
</div>
</section>

Part two · purpose
{: .eyebrow}

## Three jobs history has been given
{: .main-point}

<div class="cards three" markdown="1">
<div class="card" markdown="1">

###### Livy · *Ab Urbe Condita*
{: .num}

#### A monument to choose from

The past set out as examples: "choose for yourself... what to imitate," and mark what to avoid.
</div>
<div class="card" markdown="1">

###### Ranke · 1824
{: .num}

#### Only what actually happened

Judging the past and instructing the present are "high offices the present work does not presume."
</div>
<div class="card" markdown="1">

###### Thompson · 1963
{: .num}

#### A rescue

The stockinger and the hand-loom weaver, pulled back "from the enormous condescension of posterity."
</div>
</div>

Quoted phrases: Livy, Preface (Foster trans.); Ranke, 1824 introduction (GHDI trans.); Thompson, *The Making of the English Working Class*, p. 12.
{: .cite}

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="0" markdown="1">

**Ranke refuses the job Livy took for granted. A century later Thompson hands history a new one.**
</div>
</section>

<!-- ========== ONE FILE, TWO HISTORIANS ========== -->
<section markdown="1">
Part two · the trial record
{: .eyebrow}

## Ranke and Ginzburg, reading the same trial record
{: .main-point}
<div class="rule"></div>

<div class="reveal-block historical fragment compact" data-fragment-index="0" markdown="1">

###### The document
{: .label}

Ginzburg's source is an inquisition trial record — a state document of exactly the kind Ranke built his method on. Ranke could have read this file.
</div>

<div class="reveal-block argument fragment compact" data-fragment-index="1" markdown="1">

###### What each one reads it for
{: .label}

Ranke reads it for what the tribunal did, and gets a procedure carried out. Ginzburg reads the same pages for what the miller believed, and gets a cosmos made of cheese.
</div>

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="2" markdown="1">

**What each one wants from the page settles it before the document does. Both are reading carefully; they just get different books.**
</div>
</section>

<!-- ========== DISCUSSION · PURPOSE ========== -->
<section markdown="1">
Part two · discussion
{: .eyebrow}

## Which purpose is defensible
{: .main-point}

---

Livy wants moral examples. Voltaire wants civilizational lessons. Ranke wants truth. Marx wants revolution. Thompson wants to rescue the forgotten. Which purpose do you find most defensible, and why?
{: .question .fragment data-fragment-index="1"}

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="2" markdown="1">
These aren't always compatible goals — they produce different histories from the same sources. Pick one and you have mostly decided in advance what you can notice.
</div>
</section>

<!-- ========== TAKE HOME ========== -->
<section data-transition="fade" markdown="1">
The take home
{: .eyebrow}

## You cannot measure progress until you say what history is for
{: .main-point}
<div class="rule"></div>

<div class="reveal-block unpack fragment compact" data-fragment-index="0" markdown="1">

###### What did improve
{: .label}

The tests got stricter and the evidence got wider: who counts as a source, what counts as proof, whose past can be known at all.
</div>

<div class="reveal-block argument fragment compact" data-fragment-index="1" markdown="1">

###### What did not
{: .label}

Every writer we read decides which facts get the floor, and most of them believe the decision is justified.
</div>

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="2" markdown="1">

**Ask whether history has progressed and you have already been asked what it is for. This course has five answers to that question, and no agreed way to rank them.**
</div>
</section>
