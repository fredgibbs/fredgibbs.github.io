## Four corrections in a row
{: .main-point}

---

The course is built as a sequence of turns, each claiming to fix something the one before it missed. Ranke corrects Voltaire's speculation; Marx corrects Ranke's elitism; Thompson corrects Marx's abstraction; Ginzburg corrects Thompson's anonymity.
{: .detail}

Is there any "progress" in historical writing? Are we now closer to the truth, or just accumulating competing frameworks?
{: .question .fragment data-fragment-index="1"}

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="2" markdown="1">
Each turn fixes a specific complaint against the one before it — real progress on a narrow question. Nothing in the sequence settles the big one.
</div>
</section>

<section markdown="1">
Discussion · 2 of 5

## What history is for
{: .main-point}

---

Livy wants moral examples. Voltaire wants civilizational lessons. Ranke wants truth. Marx wants revolution. Thompson wants to rescue the forgotten.
{: .detail}

Which purpose do you find most defensible, and why?
{: .question .fragment data-fragment-index="1"}

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="2" markdown="1">
These aren't always compatible goals — they produce different histories even from the same sources. Pick one for a Café character and you have decided what that character can even notice.
</div>
</section>

## One file, two methods
{: .main-point}

---

Ginzburg's source is an inquisition trial record — a state document of exactly the kind Ranke built his method on. Ranke could have read this file. Why would he have found nothing in it?
{: .question .fragment data-fragment-index="1"}

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="2" markdown="1">
The method settles the question before the document does. Same page, and Ranke is reading for what the tribunal did while Ginzburg is reading for what the miller believed.
</div>
</section>

## Our own preselected record
{: .main-point}

---

Carr says our picture of the past was preselected by people who thought those facts worth preserving. This reading list is a selection too. What has it selected for, and whose answer to "what makes history happen" is missing because of it?
{: .question .fragment data-fragment-index="1"}

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="2" markdown="1">
The list is weighted toward writers who argued about method in writing, which is itself a rule about whose past can be known — the argument Thompson and Scott make, turned on the syllabus that assigned them.
</div>
</section>

## A method you could run
{: .main-point}

---

Say you had one document and a week. Whose method from these six weeks could you actually run on it — and whose turns out to need a lifetime, a language, or an archive nobody has?
{: .question .fragment data-fragment-index="1"}

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="2" markdown="1">
Ranke's test and Ginzburg's single file scale down to one person with one document; Braudel's centuries and Marx's whole mode of production do not. A method you cannot run is a position you are borrowing.
</div>
</section>

Historians Café · the hidden part

## Nobody says what they think history is for
{: .main-point}
<div class="rule"></div>

<div class="reveal-block unpack fragment compact" data-fragment-index="0" markdown="1">

###### What people actually say
{: .label}

"It's just history, you can't erase it." "My grandfather is on that wall." "There's no proof any of that happened." "Whose idea was it to put him up there in the first place?"
</div>

<div class="reveal-block argument fragment compact" data-fragment-index="1" markdown="1">

###### What each of them is claiming
{: .label}

That the record judges nobody. That the past belongs to the people descended from it. That only documents count. That somebody chose. Four positions from this course, in a city council meeting, with no names on them.
</div>

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="2" markdown="1">

**A value is hidden not because it is concealed, but because the person holding it thinks it is obvious.**
</div>
</section>

<!-- ========== FOUR TELLS ========== -->
<section data-transition="fade" markdown="1">
Historians Café · how to hear it
{: .eyebrow}

## Four tells in what somebody says about the past
{: .main-point}
<div class="rule"></div>

<div class="cards" markdown="1">
<div class="card" markdown="1">

###### 01
{: .num}

#### What counts as proof

Watch what they reach for. A document? A memory? What everyone knows? That choice is the whole argument between Ranke and Thompson, in one sentence from a stranger.
</div>
<div class="card" markdown="1">

###### 02
{: .num}

#### What they leave out

Who is missing from their version, and do they notice? Scott's point works on a city council speech as well as it works on the Congress of Berlin.
</div>
<div class="card" markdown="1">

###### 03
{: .num}

#### What the past is supposed to do

Teach a lesson, settle a claim, explain why things are the way they are, give somebody their due. Livy, Machiavelli, Marx and Thompson are all in that list.
</div>
<div class="card" markdown="1">

###### 04
{: .num}

#### What they treat as settled

The thing they never argue for. That is usually where the value is — and where the other person's whole case lands.
</div>
</div>
</section>

<!-- ========== THE SUMMARY TRAP ========== -->
<section markdown="1">
Historians Café · step four
{: .eyebrow}

## Explaining is not recapping
{: .main-point}
<div class="rule"></div>

<div class="reveal-block historical fragment compact" data-fragment-index="0" markdown="1">

###### The version that fails
{: .label}

"First she said the statue should come down, then he said it shouldn't, then she said..." Your friend can read the dialogue. They are asking you what it was *about*.
</div>

<div class="reveal-block argument fragment compact" data-fragment-index="1" markdown="1">

###### The version that works
{: .label}

Quote one line from each person and say what it tells you about what they think history is for. Then say which one convinced you — and what it would take to change your mind.
</div>

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="2" markdown="1">

**They were arguing about a statue. Say what they were actually disagreeing about, and you have done the thing this course has been teaching for seven weeks.**
</div>
</section>

## What changed, and what didn't
{: .main-point}
<div class="rule"></div>

<div class="cards three" markdown="1">
<div class="card" markdown="1">

###### What history is
{: .num}

#### Selection, always

Carr's facts never spoke alone; Ranke's documents needed a chooser too. What changed is who counts as worth choosing.
</div>
<div class="card" markdown="1">

###### How to do it
{: .num}

#### Evidence widened, method didn't relax

Al-Biruni named his sources; Ranke made the archive the test; Thompson and Vansina made memory and oral tradition count as evidence too.
</div>
<div class="card" markdown="1">

###### What it's for
{: .num}

#### From lesson to explanation to rescue

Livy taught virtue, Kant found a plan, Marx found a motor. Thompson and Scott went looking for who each method leaves out.
</div>
</div>
</section>

<section data-transition="fade" markdown="1">
The take home · six weeks
{: .eyebrow}

## The record was never neutral, and the disagreement moved, not disappeared
{: .main-point}
<div class="rule"></div>

<div class="reveal-block unpack fragment compact" data-fragment-index="0" markdown="1">

###### What stayed constant
{: .label}

Every writer we've read decides which facts get to speak, and every one of them believes the decision is justified.
</div>

<div class="reveal-block argument fragment compact" data-fragment-index="1" markdown="1">

###### What actually changed
{: .label}

Who got to be a fact: kings and wars, then structures, then class as lived experience, then gender, then a single trial.
</div>

<div class="takehome fragment compact" data-fragment-index="2" markdown="1">

**Six weeks moved the argument from what happened to who gets to count as having happened at all.**
</div>
</section>
