## The diagrams

The first five `diagram-*.png` files are measurements, not illustrations. Every
probability on them was produced for this deck on 2026-10-27, on a laptop CPU,
by two small open models:

- **GPT-2** (`openai-community/gpt2`, 124M parameters, OpenAI 2019), run in float32.
- **OLMo 2 1B** (`allenai/OLMo-2-0425-1B`, about 1.5B parameters, Ai2 2025), run in bfloat16 with the softmax in float32.

Both used Hugging Face `transformers` 5.21 and torch 2.14 under Python 3.13.
Commercial chatbots are far larger, and tuned, so the numbers show the
mechanism rather than what ChatGPT would say.

**Three scripts** produced them, kept in `../_diagram-scripts/` along with the
reasoning diagram's scripts. The leading underscore keeps Jekyll from
publishing the folder. The scripts first lived in a temporary scratchpad,
which was emptied on 2026-10-28 when the disk filled. They were restored
verbatim from the session record, but the probe output (`probe-*.json`,
`years-*.json`) was lost. The numbers on the diagrams are as printed at the
time, and a rerun is needed to see them again.

- `probe.py MODEL_ID`: the ten most probable next tokens for each prompt, plus
  the probability of named tokens (" he", " she", " France", " Haiti", …).
  It also tokenized the two example sentences and drew three samples per model
  from the Zimmerman prompt: `torch.manual_seed(1105)`, temperature 0.8,
  top-p 0.95, 30 new tokens.
- `years.py MODEL_ID`: scores every whole year from " 1850" to " 1960" after
  the UNM prompt. It multiplies the token probabilities along each year's
  tokenization, so that GPT-2 (one token per year) and OLMo (" ", "188", "9")
  can be compared. The result: 1889 ranks first for GPT-2 (1.7%) and 23rd for
  OLMo (0.7%).
- `diagrams.py`: writes each diagram as an HTML page, 1200 px wide, in the
  deck's palette. Headless Chrome screenshotted them at device scale 2, and
  they were resized to 1800 px wide.

To rebuild, install `transformers` and `torch`, run the two probes for each
model ID, run `diagrams.py`, then screenshot each page:

```
chrome --headless=new --force-device-scale-factor=2 --window-size=1200,<h> --screenshot=…
```

The heights are tokens 600, next-token 590, three-runs 700, 1791 540 and
pronouns 540. The prompts are in `probe.py` and appear verbatim on each
diagram. Re-running with the same seed and library versions reproduces the
samples. A different torch version may change them.

## The reasoning diagram

`diagram-reasoning.png` shows one run of **Qwen3 1.7B** (`Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B`,
Alibaba, April 2025) in thinking mode. It was run in bfloat16 on the laptop's
GPU (MPS) with the same library versions as the other diagrams. Settings:
Qwen's recommended sampling for thinking mode (temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95,
top-k 20), up to 1,500 new tokens, `torch.manual_seed(1105)`, and no search or
tools. The question was "When was Zimmerman Library at the University of New
Mexico built?"

The run wrote 497 tokens of notes and a 94-token answer. The answer says 1952
and names "H. H. Richardson & Company" as architect. The library was begun in
December 1936 and opened in April 1938 to a design by John Gaw Meem.
Richardson died in 1886. Four more runs (seeds 1106–1109) answered 1939,
1934, 1961 and 1923; the diagram's footnote says so. A second prompt in the
same run, "How many r's are in the word 'strawberry'?", spelled the word out
and answered three. It is not on a slide.

The diagram excerpts five passages of the notes and two of the answer.
`diagram_reasoning.py` asserts that each is a verbatim substring of the saved
output, and the diagram marks every cut with "…". A sixth excerpt, an
invented "H. R. Lyman Library, which was built in 1955", was dropped to make
room for larger text. The violet phrases are the notes' talk of checking. The
rose phrases are the answer's false claims.

Qwen3's small models were not trained by reinforcement learning on their own.
They were distilled from Qwen3-32B and Qwen3-235B (Qwen3 Technical Report,
arXiv 2505.09388, §4.5). The caption's "Reasoning is trained on questions with
a checkable answer" describes how such teachers are trained. DeepSeek-R1
(arXiv 2501.12948, p. 2) states it plainly: the reward "is solely based on the
correctness of final predictions against ground-truth answers."

The scripts are `reason.py` (both prompts, seed 1105), `reason_more.py`
(seeds 1106–1109) and `diagram_reasoning.py`, which reuses the page style in
`diagrams.py`. The diagram was screenshotted at 1200×620 and device scale 2,
then resized to 1800 px wide. The scripts and their output
(`reason-Qwen3-1.7B.json`, `reason-more-Qwen3-1.7B.json`) are in
`../_diagram-scripts/`.

## Notes

**The Trouillot cover is fair use, and small.** No freely licensed photograph
of Trouillot (1949–2012) turned up on Commons. A search of the University of
Chicago's obituary pages found none either. The deck uses the book instead,
because its cover shows the Citadelle Laferrière, the fortress Henri
Christophe built in post-revolutionary Haiti, and that ties the image to the
argument. Open Library holds three Beacon covers:

- 587956 and the ISBN copy, 325×475;
- 12840723, 326×500;
- 12793881, a 2018 printing, 317×500.

All three are about 500 px tall, so none is meaningfully sharper, and the
ISBN copy of the 1995 edition is used. The cover is shown
at 262×382 to identify an assigned reading, in a non-commercial teaching deck.
That is 1.24× the display size, under the 1.5× target; `deck-check.sh` WARNs
on it. Recorded in `../ISSUES.md`.

**The Gurnee and Tegmark figure is fair use.** It is one panel of a figure from
a research paper, shown at reduced size beside a quotation from the same paper
so the class can see what "a map inside a model" means. Nothing freely licensed
shows the same thing. The caption cites the figure.

**The Vesuvius licence is uncertain, so the caption uses the stricter one.**
The Commons file page says CC BY-SA 4.0, credited to scrollprize.org. The
Vesuvius Challenge site says "Content licensed CC BY-NC 4.0 unless otherwise
specified." The caption carries CC BY-NC 4.0, which is satisfied by a
non-commercial teaching deck. Recorded in `../ISSUES.md`.

**The Gebru photograph was taken by a Getty photographer for TechCrunch.**
TechCrunch released it on Flickr under CC BY 2.0. The caption credits
TechCrunch, as the licence's attribution names it.

**The Racknitz plates are his reconstruction, not the machine.** Plate III is
Racknitz's guess at where the operator sat, and he placed him wrongly. The
image-notes slide says so; the bleed slides' alt text describes what the plate
shows, not how the Turk worked.
