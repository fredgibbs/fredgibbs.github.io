## Notes

**The dam is an illustration, not the reading's example.** Maza (p. 110) quotes
Paul Sutter on a dammed western river as an "organic machine"; that phrase is
the title of Richard White's 1995 book on the Columbia, so the slide shows the
Columbia's largest dam. Neither White nor Grand Coulee is named in the reading.

**The Catlin shows one of Cronon's five horses.** His lesson (p. 9) lists an
English colonial, a Spanish conquistador, a Comanche trading, a Sioux raiding
and a Pawnee herding horse. Catlin painted Comanche riders in 1834–35.

**Coalbrookdale stands for the date, not for a claim in the reading.**
Chakrabarty (p. 209) quotes Crutzen dating the Anthropocene to the latter part
of the eighteenth century and Watt's steam engine of 1784. The painting is of
Shropshire iron furnaces in 1801; the reading does not mention it.

**Four illustrations added in the second round are not in the readings:**
*Earthrise* stands for the environmental movement of about 1970; Muir and
Roosevelt for the wilderness cause Nash wrote the history of (Maza names Muir,
p. 109); *The Oxbow* for cleared and uncleared New England, fifty years after
the period *Changes in the Land* covers; the Wardang Island rabbits for Maza's
"proliferating livestock such as rabbits" in Australia (p. 114).
