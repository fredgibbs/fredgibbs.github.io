# Open issues — The 1619 Project, Part 2 (Week 12.2)

Contributor notes, excluded from the build. Deliberate deviations are recorded
here so a later review does not raise them again.

## What this deck is

The fight over the essay, organized by argument rather than by author. Built
on 2026-11-02 when the combined Week 12 deck was split (the request is quoted
in `../1619-project/ISSUES.md`). The author asked for three things: the main
pro and con arguments stated clearly, the five historians' letter highlighted,
and balance, "so they feel different perspectives are represented."

**Order.** Title; throughline; Hannah-Jones's portrait; the four claims in
dispute. Part one, the letter and the reply: the letter as a thing, "fact, not
framing," Douglass, the letter's three graded objections, its objection to the
Times's authority and what it asked, the editor's reply (including his answer
on the process), discussion. Part two, the disputes, each a `.parallel` with
one side per track: the Revolution (with Serwer's verdict), Dunmore's
proclamation, "alone" (with Serwer's verdict), who won freedom, the "DNA"
line, Du Bois, the "white historians" charge, objectivity, and Friedersdorf as
a third position on the founding date; discussion. Then the case for and the
case against (four cards each), the take home, and the writing slide.

## How balance is kept

- **Each dispute gets one slide with both sides in equal tracks.** Where a
  verdict follows, it is Serwer's (the narrator), reported in the direction he
  gives it: he finds the critics "on firm ground" on the Revolution and adds
  that historians in neither camp "say both have a point"; on "alone" he leans
  to Hannah-Jones ("more than defensible"). No verdict is added where Serwer
  gives none.
- **Every objection in the letter has its answer on screen**: the Revolution
  (Somerset, Dunmore, Waldstreicher), "alone" (Hannah-Jones to Serwer),
  Lincoln (the reply on his colonization plans), "white historians" (the
  tweet in context), the process (no formal panel; checked "with subject-area
  experts"), and schools (Silverstein to Serwer: "supplementary").
- **The two summary slides are built to match.** Card 1 on each side is the
  fact-or-interpretation question; the Revolution, Black struggle against the
  founding's ideals, and schools follow on both. The case against's fourth card
  is Friedersdorf, labelled as someone "who agrees with much of the essay," and
  his slide's eyebrow calls him "a third position," as the schedule does.
- **Concessions** are in the take home. In the readings only the project's side
  concedes a point of fact (Hannah-Jones on her wording; the reply on
  Somerset's legal reach), so the takehome no longer says "each side could
  concede a fact."
- **Part one is the letter's.** Five slides give its objections in its own
  words, as the author asked; the reply gets one slide there, and a full track
  on each dispute in part two.
- **Serwer is a track on the "DNA" slide.** He is the narrator but also the
  project's main defender on that point, and Oakes is quoted through him. The
  readings hold no Times or Hannah-Jones answer to Oakes. The headline was
  changed from "changed or endured" to "how much racism has changed," since
  Serwer's side ("fragile and reversible") grants change.

## Facts that rest on something other than the readings

- **The March 2020 editor's note** (take home: the Times "revised the sentence
  to cover some colonists, not all"). Paraphrased, not quoted, because the note
  itself was not open. Source: a secondary report (iMediaEthics), which quotes
  it as "A passage has been adjusted to make clear that a desire to protect
  slavery was among the motivations of some of the colonists who fought the
  Revolutionary War, not among the motivations of all of them." Opening the
  Times's own page would let the slide quote it.
- **"Five senior historians"** and **"four other leading historians"**: the
  signers' titles in the letter (distinguished and emeritus professors at
  Princeton, Brown, CUNY and Texas State) and Serwer's "all leading scholars in
  their field."
- **"They sent it on December 4"** is Serwer's date; "a version on December
  20" is the Times page's.
- **Dunmore's proclamation**: the date is on the broadside; "Virginia's royal
  governor" is the broadside's own title for him ("His Majesty's Lieutenant and
  Governor General of the Colony and Dominion of Virginia").
- **Douglass's and Du Bois's captions**: "born enslaved in Maryland," "best-known
  Black abolitionist" and *Black Reconstruction*'s date (1935) are general
  knowledge. What Du Bois challenged is quoted from Serwer.
- **"The project itself spoke of 'understanding 1619 as our true founding'"**
  (claims slide): the project's framing as Friedersdorf quotes it. It is not in
  the essay.

## Deliberate choices

- **Speakers quoted through other readings** are named with the reading in the
  track heading: Wood "quoted in Friedersdorf," Oakes "to the World Socialist
  Web Site, in Serwer," Wilentz and Hannah-Jones "to Serwer." Wood and Oakes are
  introduced only as "a signer"; their names are on the title slide.
- **Single-appearance names** (Calhoun in the letter's quotation, Waldstreicher
  in the reply's, the Southern Poverty Law Center) stay: each is the speaker of
  the thing quoted.
- **The letter's graded words** ("not true," "distorted," "misleading") are its
  own: "This is not true," "Some of the other material in the project is
  distorted," "Still other material is misleading."
- **The letter's Revolution quotation of the essay** ("in order to ensure
  slavery would continue") is not the essay's exact wording ("independence was
  required in order to ensure that slavery would continue," p. 18). The
  objections card quotes the essay, then the letter's verdict.
- **"Founded on racial slavery"** is the letter's description of the project.
  The essay's own sentence is hedged: "some might argue that this nation was
  founded not as a democracy but as a slavocracy" (p. 18). The "who won freedom"
  slide gives the letter's words in the letter's track and does not add the
  hedge; a reader who wants it should know it is there.
- **Parts do not open with a `.detail` line** (guide, "Structure," item 4).
  Each part's first slide is a quote slide or a `.parallel`, where a `.detail`
  above the quotation fails the order check; the eyebrow names the part.
- **No Silverstein, Serwer, Friedersdorf or signer portraits**: see
  `images/README.md`.
- **The writing slide asks students to weigh both sides before picking one**,
  which reconciles the post page (weigh) with the schedule's alert (pick); see
  the open issue in `../1619-project/ISSUES.md`.
- **Not covered: the Week 12.2 Trouillot question about the project being banned
  from state curricula.** No reading in hand speaks to it.
