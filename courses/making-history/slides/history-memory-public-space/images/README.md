# Slide Images — History, Memory, and Public Space (11.1)

Nineteen image files, all used by `../index.md`. Every photograph is public
domain or under a Creative Commons license; the Mapping Inequality slide is a
screenshot of a website, used as fair use (the author, 2026-10-23: "everything
is fair use for a class lecture"). Three licenses carry conditions worth
knowing before reuse: Loewen's portrait is **BY-NC-SA** (non-commercial), and
Hayden's portrait and the Estrada Courts mural photograph are **BY-NC-ND**
(non-commercial, no derivatives, so both are used unmodified). Credits are
carried in the `<figcaption>`s and should not be stripped.

The folder totals about 6.6 MB, under the guide's 10 MB. The chapter 1 images
(Baldwin, Penn Station, the African Burial Ground) went when that part was cut
on 2026-10-23, and nine of the eleven "who put this here?" images went when
that part was cut later the same day (see Notes).

## Notes

**Hayden's portrait.** The only free photographs found are two Flickr frames:
Dean Terry's (BY-NC-ND, 716×477) and Liz Henry's (BY-ND, 640×480, a dim
gallery reading). Both originals are private on Flickr, so nothing larger is
available. A 716-pixel frame fails `deck-check.sh` on a portrait or landscape
image slide, so she is introduced on a `.with-figure.wide` explainer instead,
where the image displays about 300px wide. The ND license rules out cropping
the letterbox bars. A Yale or MIT Press photograph would be fair use; none was
pursued.

**The three illustrations of Hayden's three histories.** Only the Chamisal
photograph (earlier in the deck) is one Hayden prints. Her working-landscapes
figure, 2.1, is Chinese American railroad workers on a handcar beside an Anglo
American supervisor near Lang, California, 1876 (Security Pacific Collection,
Los Angeles Public Library; the Oakland Museum of California holds a print).
No free copy was found on Commons or at the Library of Congress, so the Wrights
Tunnel photograph stands in: it is the tunnel she names on p. 21. The Belzoni
photograph is not hers either: she discusses segregated entrances and balconies
as territorial history (p. 24) but prints Los Angeles pictures (figs. 2.2,
2.3). Her building-types figure, 2.9, is a Lower East Side street of
tenements, ca. 1900 (Library of Congress); the Tenement Museum interior goes
with her p. 33 sentence about standing inside one.

**The Wrights Tunnel photograph** is 1393 pixels wide, the largest copy found;
`deck-check.sh` passes it without a warning. The Commons title says "north
portal"; santacruztrains.com's caption says the barracks stood "near the
Summit Tunnel's western portal." The slide says only "by Wrights Tunnel."

**The counter-space photographs.** Hayden prints a casita (fig. 2.10, Martha
Cooper, 1989) and two Estrada Courts murals (figs. 2.11, 2.12, her own, 1980:
the Virgin of Guadalupe and a woman with a machine gun). The course scan has
them as grayscale halftones about 1,150px wide, and in fig. 2.10 the casita is
a speck among tenements, so color photographs of the same places stand in.
Commons had nothing usable for either. Openverse found the Flickr sets; Flickr
itself refused this machine ("Rate limited - CIDR range blocked") on
2026-10-23, so both files came through Openverse's image proxy
(`api.openverse.org/v1/images/<id>/thumb/?full_size=true&compressed=false`),
which serves Flickr's 1024px size. The casita draws a `deck-check.sh` WARN
(1.48x). Re-fetching the Flickr originals would clear it. No Estrada Courts
photograph of Hayden's two murals turned up; Calisphere's 1979 Cinewest slides
(UC San Diego) blocked scripted access.

**The Mapping Prejudice map** came from the University Digital Conservancy,
which refuses plain `curl` (403); it was fetched from inside a headless Chrome
tab on the item page.

**Dropped.** `stone-mountain-carving.jpg` (Jim Bowen, 2012, CC BY 2.0) went
with the redo of 2026-10-23, when Loewen's part was cut to its main points.
When the author cut the "who put this here?" part the same day, nine images
went with it: the Santa Fe Soldiers' Monument north panel, its 1973 plaque and
its boxed base; the Lee Monument in 1890 and 2020; the *La Jornada* and *Numbe
Whageh* plaques; Mount Rushmore; and the Crazy Horse Memorial. All came from
Wikimedia Commons or VCU Libraries. The two Hodgin Hall images stayed.

**Searched and not used.** No usable photograph of the Orville Hubbard marker
or statue on Commons (Loewen, p. 20). Commons' Ethan Allen monument photo is
360×580. The 1930 booklet *Historical Markers Erected by Massachusetts Bay
Colony Tercentenary Commission* (Internet Archive `historicalmarker00mass`)
prints every marker text; the IA scans are faded and the Commons PDF renders
at 500px, so the Grafton marker photograph stands in for it. No Commons image
was found of the Kenneth Adams murals in UNM's Zimmerman Library, which would
sit well beside Hodgin Hall if a licensed photograph turns up.
